Candidate answer: 3M's operating income margin fell 1.7 percentage points, from 20.8% of net sales in FY2021 to 19.1% in FY2022. Operating margin is a useful metric for 3M (a diversified industrial manufacturer), and the change decomposes as follows (all as a percent of net sales): (1) Cost of sales rose 3.0 pp, to 56.2% from 53.2%, driven by 2022 special-item costs for significant litigation from additional commitments on PFAS-related matters at the Zwijndrecht, Belgium site, higher raw materials and logistics costs, manufacturing productivity headwinds magnified by the shutdown of certain Belgium operations and restarting previously-idled operations, and investments in growth, productivity and sustainability — partially offset by higher selling prices. (2) SG&A rose 6.1 pp, to 26.5% from 20.4%, driven by increased special-item litigation costs, primarily a ~$1.2 billion pre-tax Q2 2022 charge related to steps toward resolving Combat Arms Earplugs litigation, impairment costs related to exiting PFAS manufacturing, costs of exiting Russia, divestiture-related restructuring charges, and continued investment in key growth initiatives — partially offset by restructuring benefits and ongoing cost management. (3) Goodwill impairment expense added 0.8 pp (new in 2022), a charge on the Advanced Materials reporting unit tied to the commitment to exit PFAS manufacturing. Partially offsetting these headwinds: (4) a gain on business divestitures of 8.0 pp of sales (an 8.0 pp benefit), reflecting the Q3 2022 pre-tax gain of $2.7 billion ($2.7 billion after tax) on the split-off and combination of the Food Safety business with Neogen Corporation, and (5) R&D declined 0.2 pp, to 5.4% from 5.6%. Ongoing worldwide ERP deployment investments also affected cost of sales, SG&A and R&D. Net effect: cost of sales +3.0, SG&A +6.1, goodwill impairment +0.8, R&D -0.2, divestiture gain -8.0 = 1.7 pp of margin compression.
