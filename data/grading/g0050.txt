Question: What drove operating margin change as of the FY22 for AMD? If operating margin is not a useful metric for a company like this, then please state that and explain why.

Gold answer: The decrease in AMD's operating income was primarily driven by amortization of intangible assets associated with the Xilinx acquisition

Candidate answer: AMD's GAAP operating margin collapsed in FY22 (fiscal year ended Dec 31, 2022) to roughly 5% — about $1.3 billion of operating income on $23.6 billion of revenue — down from roughly 22% in FY21 (about $3.6 billion on $16.4 billion). That is a decline on the order of 17 percentage points, and essentially all of it is acquisition accounting rather than a collapse in the underlying business.

Main drivers:
1. Amortization of acquisition-related intangibles from the Xilinx (closed Feb 2022) and Pensando deals — roughly $3.5 billion of amortization in FY22 versus a negligible amount (~$0.1 billion) in FY21. This flows through both cost of sales (pulling GAAP gross margin down to ~45% from ~48%) and operating expenses, and by itself accounts for the great majority of the margin decline.
2. Other acquisition-related and integration charges, plus a large step-up in stock-based compensation (roughly $1.1–1.4 billion) from Xilinx retention and converted awards.
3. A much larger operating expense base: R&D rose to about $5.0 billion from about $2.8 billion, and marketing/G&A also grew, as the Xilinx headcount was consolidated — opex grew faster than revenue in 2H22 when revenue growth stalled.
4. Deterioration in the Client (PC) segment in 2H22 — the PC market downturn caused a sharp revenue decline, inventory/pricing charges, and Client segment operating income fell to roughly break-even/negative for the year, dragging the consolidated mix.

Partial offsets: revenue grew about 44% year over year, and the Data Center and the newly added Embedded (Xilinx) segments carried very high segment operating margins (Embedded around 50%+, Data Center around 30%), which is why non-GAAP operating margin held up at roughly 27% versus about 27% in FY21. The GAAP-vs-non-GAAP gap is the clearest evidence that the FY22 margin decline was driven by purchase-accounting amortization and stock-based compensation rather than by core operating deterioration — though the Client segment weakness in the second half was a genuine business drag.

Operating margin is a useful metric for AMD (a fabless semiconductor company with real product revenue and operating leverage), but for FY22 specifically the GAAP figure is distorted by Xilinx purchase accounting, so non-GAAP operating margin and segment-level operating income are the more informative reads.
