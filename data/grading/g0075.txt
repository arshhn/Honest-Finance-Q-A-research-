Question: How are dividends taxed for a Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT)?

Gold answer: Dividends from REITs are generally not eligible for the preferential tax rates applicable to qualified dividend income, meaning they are taxed at the shareholder's ordinary income tax rate.

Candidate answer: The REIT is generally not taxed at the corporate level on income it distributes to shareholders, provided it distributes at least 90% of its REIT taxable income (excluding net capital gains) each year. Shareholders are taxed on the dividends they receive. Under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (Code Section 199A), individual taxpayers and estates/trusts may deduct 20% of the aggregate amount of 'qualified REIT dividends' from their taxable income, effectively lowering the tax on that portion; qualified REIT dividends exclude any portion of a dividend classified as a capital gain dividend or as qualified dividend income, which are taxed under separate rules. Any taxable income the REIT fails to distribute (including undistributed net capital gains) is taxed to the REIT itself at regular corporate rates, though properly designated undistributed capital gains avoid taxation at the shareholder level.
