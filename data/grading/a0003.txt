Question: What drove gross margin change as of the FY2022 for American Express? If gross margin is not a useful metric for a company like this, then please state that and explain why.

Gold answer: Performance is not measured through gross margin

Candidate answer: Gross margin is not a useful or meaningful metric for American Express, and the company does not report it (no "cost of goods sold" or gross profit line appears in its FY2022 income statement). Amex is an integrated payments company — a card network plus a bank holding company — so its economics are not production-and-sale of goods. Its income statement runs from total revenues net of interest expense (discount revenue, net card fees, service fees, processed revenue, and net interest income) down through provisions for credit losses and operating expenses (including card member rewards, card member services, business development, and marketing) to pre-tax income. There is no meaningful separation of "cost of revenue" from operating expense, so any gross-margin figure would be an arbitrary construct.

The relevant FY2022 profitability drivers instead were: total revenues net of interest expense of roughly $52.9 billion, up about 25% year over year, driven by record Card Member spending as travel and entertainment recovered post-pandemic, higher net card fee revenue from premium fee-based product growth, and sharply higher net interest income as loan balances rebounded and rates rose. Offsetting this, provisions for credit losses swung to roughly $2.2 billion (from a large reserve release in 2021) as reserves were rebuilt on growing loan balances, and expenses rose on higher rewards, Card Member travel/services usage, and marketing investment. Net income was about $7.5 billion. Appropriate margin/return metrics for a company like this are pre-tax margin, net income margin on revenues net of interest expense, net interest margin, return on equity (about 30% in 2022), and efficiency ratio — not gross margin.
