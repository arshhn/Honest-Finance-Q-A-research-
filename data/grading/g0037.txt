A bank/financial holding company like JPM does not report cost of goods sold or gross profit in its income statement; JPM's income statement is structured around net interest income (interest income less interest expense) plus noninterest revenue (investment banking fees, trading/principal transactions, asset management fees, card income, etc.), against a provision for credit losses and noninterest expense (compensation, occupancy, technology, legal). There is no COGS line, so no gross margin can be computed, and any figure derived by treating interest expense as "cost of revenue" would be an artifact of definition rather than an economically meaningful margin (and would swing widely with the interest-rate cycle rather than with operating efficiency). So the premise of the question (whether gross margins vary by more than ~2% per year) cannot be assessed for JPM.
