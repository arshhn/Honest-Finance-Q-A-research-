Candidate answer: Rethinking Power Conversion: Inside Ideal Power's Technology Portfolio

Every solar panel, battery bank, and microgrid on the planet runs into the same stubborn problem: the world generates and stores a growing share of its electricity as direct current, while the grid and most equipment still run on alternating current. Bridging that gap takes power conversion hardware, and Austin, Texas-based Ideal Power Inc. has spent more than a decade arguing that the conventional way of building it is heavier, bulkier, and less efficient than it needs to be.

At the core of the company's approach is its proprietary Power Packet Switching Architecture, or PPSA. Instead of the continuous power flow used by traditional converters, which depends on relatively heavy and expensive magnetic components and bulk capacitors, PPSA uses indirect power flow: energy passes through input switches, is briefly stored in a proprietary AC link inductor, and is then released to the output switches once it reaches the correct voltage and frequency. Fast switching algorithms move quantum packets of power between ports. The payoff is a converter that eliminates most of the passive components of conventional designs, including the separate isolation transformer, while delivering advantages in size and weight and offering built-in isolation plus bi-directional and multi-port capability. Because PPSA pairs standardized hardware with application-specific embedded software, one platform can serve on-grid and off-grid uses alike, and it underpins the company's commercial products, including the multi-port Stabiliti power conversion systems and the SunDial line, whose patented ability to be deployed first as a standard commercial PV inverter and later field-upgraded to add energy storage the company believes is unique in the market.

The more ambitious project sits one layer down, at the semiconductor itself. Today's products rely on insulated gate bipolar transistors (IGBTs), which switch power in only one direction and need blocking diodes, forcing Ideal Power to double up components to achieve bi-directional conversion, with each added device introducing voltage drops and waste heat. The company's answer is the B-TRAN, a bi-directional bipolar transistor designed to replace two pairs of IGBTs and diodes with a single switch. Progress has been real: after a semiconductor fabricator successfully tested single-sided B-TRAN silicon dies in 2016, the company completed proof-of-concept testing of double-sided B-TRAN prototypes in the first quarter of 2018, with test results measuring B-TRAN electrical losses at less than 40% of those of conventional switches such as silicon IGBTs, and validating that the devices can be made with conventional silicon fabrication equipment and processes.

Backing all of this is an intellectual property position the company treats as central to its business: as of December 31, 2017, PPSA and the bi-directional switch technologies were protected by a portfolio of 66 US and 12 foreign issued patents, including 38 US and five foreign patents tied to PPSA and 28 US and seven foreign patents covering the operation, control, and manufacturing of the B-TRAN. With a strategy that begins with selling PPSA-based products into the solar-plus-storage and microgrid markets and aims to expand through licensing to OEMs and eventually into the multi-billion dollar power semiconductor market, Ideal Power is betting that better switching is the foundation for a cleaner, more flexible grid.
