Answer A: No — CVS Health is not a capital-intensive business based on FY2022 data. Net property and equipment was only $12,873 million, or about 5.6% of total assets of $228,275 million, and equal to just ~4.0% of total revenues of $322,467 million (revenue/net PP&E of roughly 25x). Total asset turnover was about 1.4x ($322,467M revenue / $228,275M total assets). The balance sheet is dominated by intangible-type and working-capital/insurance items rather than fixed assets — goodwill of $78,150 million and intangible assets, net of $24,754 million together are about $102,904 million (~45% of total assets), plus accounts receivable of $27,276 million and inventories of $19,090 million. CVS also leases rather than owns most of its store base (operating lease right-of-use assets of $17,872 million, well above owned PP&E). So CVS is better characterized as a low-fixed-asset, high-turnover retail/pharmacy-services and insurance business, though it is balance-sheet heavy overall due to goodwill and intangibles from acquisitions.

Answer B: No. CVS Health is not a capital-intensive business. At December 31, 2022, net property and equipment was only $12,873 million out of $228,275 million of total assets (about 5.6%), while the balance sheet is dominated by receivables, inventories, goodwill and intangibles. Its FY2022 cost structure is driven by cost of products sold ($196,892 million) and benefit costs ($71,281 million) on $322,467 million of revenues, which is characteristic of a retail/pharmacy and health-insurance services business rather than a heavy fixed-asset business.
