Question: How are dividends taxed for a Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT)?

Gold answer: Dividends from REITs are generally not eligible for the preferential tax rates applicable to qualified dividend income, meaning they are taxed at the shareholder's ordinary income tax rate.

Candidate answer: A REIT that qualifies under Sections 856-860 of the Internal Revenue Code is generally not taxed at the corporate level under Federal and certain state income tax laws on taxable income it distributes to shareholders (subject to limited exceptions), so distributed income is taxed at the shareholder level; the REIT must meet a 90% distribution requirement, and it is taxed at regular corporate rates on any undistributed taxable income, including undistributed net capital gains (properly designated undistributed capital gains effectively avoid taxation at the shareholder level). At the shareholder level, under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017, Code Section 199A (effective for tax years beginning after December 31, 2017 and before January 1, 2026) permits individual taxpayers and estates and trusts, subject to certain income limitations, to deduct 20% of the aggregate amount of qualified REIT dividends they receive from their taxable income. Qualified REIT dividends do not include any portion of a dividend classified as a capital gain dividend or qualified dividend income.
